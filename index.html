The Gap

Scattered Learning Cannot Replace Structured Study

No sequence.

Most online Islamic content is designed for access, not formation. A student who watches lectures and reads translated texts accumulates information — but without a defined sequence, that information has no structure to rest on. Topics encountered out of order cannot be properly understood, and knowledge that is not properly understood does not compound.

No depth.

The classical Islamic sciences are not accessible through summaries. Arabic grammar, usul al-fiqh, the authentication of hadith — these are mastered through direct engagement with primary texts, not through their translation or simplified explanation. A starting point is not an education.

No chain.

Knowledge in the Islamic tradition is transmitted through people, not platforms — and who taught those people matters equally. An instructor without a verified authorization offers the student no way to assess the reliability of what they are receiving. Serious students feel this absence even when they cannot fully name it.

Programs of Study

Six disciplines, each structured in sequential levels from foundational through advanced. All programs draw directly from classical Arabic sources.

  • Arabic Language

    Grammar, morphology, and applied comprehension — the necessary foundation for reading classical Islamic texts with precision and without dependency on translation.

  • Quran

    Recitation according to the rules of tajweed, structured memorization, and an introduction to the interpretive principles that govern how the text is approached.

  • Aqeedah

    A systematic study of Islamic creed: the fundamentals of belief, the major theological positions within the Sunni tradition, and how those positions are grounded in primary sources.

  • Fiqh

    Islamic jurisprudence studied within a recognized legal school, with attention to the rulings themselves, the evidence behind them, and the methodology used to derive them.

  • Hadith

    The science of Prophetic narrations: authentication, the major collections, and the methodology for drawing legal and theological conclusions from the transmitted texts.

  • Tafsir

    Classical and contemporary Quranic exegesis taught alongside the interpretive principles that govern how the text is read, understood, and applied.

Our Approach

Enrollment is granted through a structured application process. Cohorts are kept small by design, so that each student receives adequate attention and the standard of instruction remains consistently high.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is eligible to apply?
Applications are open to adult learners committed to structured, sequential study. There are no geographic restrictions. Each applicant is assessed individually on academic background and stated intentions.
Is prior knowledge of Arabic required?
Not for the Arabic Language program, which begins at the foundational level. Students applying to Fiqh, Hadith, or other programs that depend on Arabic comprehension are expected to have a working knowledge of the language, or to enroll in the Arabic program first.
How are classes conducted?
Classes are delivered online through scheduled live sessions, with assigned readings and assessments between sessions. Recordings are provided for review, but live attendance is expected.
What is the time commitment?
Students should expect a minimum of five to seven hours per week per course, including class time, reading, and review. This is structured study, not passive consumption.
What happens after I apply?
Completed applications are reviewed by the admissions team. Applicants will be contacted within two to three weeks with a decision or a request for additional information. Not all applicants are accepted.
